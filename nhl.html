How the ratings work

Every distinct date is one Glicko-2 rating period, so each rating change maps to the games played that day. Regular season and playoff games all count (switch any of them off in Settings).

Between seasons the rating carries over and the rating deviation (RD) resets to a smaller starting value, since the team's history is informative. If a franchise disappeared, all continuing teams are moved by the same amount so the league average is back at 1500. A new franchise enters at the rating that keeps the league average at 1500 (Settings lets you choose between that literal rule and first re-centring the continuing teams so entrants come in at exactly 1500).

Margin of victory enters through the game score: the winner scores 1 / (1 + e^(−margin / 2)). Home ice is worth a rating edge that is estimated each season from the previous two regular seasons only, so nothing looks ahead. Neutral-site games (the 2020 bubble) get no edge.

"Win %" in Games is the pre-game probability the home team wins, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability.

How it works

Each simulated season starts from the records so far and plays every standings game (regular season) still without a result. Win probabilities come from the current ratings plus the home edge, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability when margin scoring is on. With rating uncertainty on, each season first draws every team's true strength from its rating ± RD, so early in the year the spread of outcomes is wide and it tightens as results come in.

Teams are ranked in each conference by points (2 for a win, 1 for an overtime or shootout loss). Ties use the official order: regulation wins, wins excluding shootouts, total wins, points in games between the tied clubs, then goal differential (total goals scored is skipped). Roughly one game in four goes past regulation, as in recent seasons; those are one-goal games decided close to a coin flip, and regulation margins follow the real distribution. The playoffs take the top three of each division plus two wild cards per conference; the better division winner meets the lower wild card and every round is a best-of-seven. Odds appear in the Championship odds table.

Import schedule or results

On Hockey-Reference's season schedule page use Share & Export → Get table as CSV, then upload the file or paste the text. Plain date,away,home[,awayPts,homePts[,type]] rows work too, and so does a Type or Notes column (Playoffs). Team names, nicknames and abbreviations are all understood.

Importing merges: new games are added and scores fill in results for games you already have. Re-import any time to load new results.

Automatic updates

A scheduled job refreshes the schedule and results several times a day and publishes them with this app; the app merges them every time you open it. Results you type yourself are never overwritten, and a result you clear is filled in again from the file.

Glicko-2 parameters

RD at season start is 50 by default (a grid over 70,000 games found 30–80 about equally good; hockey results are noisy, so ratings move slowly). New franchises start at 350. Margin scales of 1.5 to 3 goals and τ of 0.5 to 1 made almost no difference, so 2 goals and 0.8 stay.

Model quality

Pre-game predictions of the home team winning, evaluated over every game that feeds the ratings. Log loss of a coin flip is 0.693.

Data and assumptions

1917-18 to 2003-04 come from the NHLData package (scores scraped from Hockey-Reference). 2005-06 onwards come from the sportsdataverse NHL schedule tables (fastRhockey data repository), with overtime taken from the goal log and shootouts from the schedule table; its 2005-06 to 2015-16 games were cross-checked against the package (same results, a few playoff games dated one day apart), and the four seasons 2005-06 to 2008-09 take their overtime and shootout flags from the package. One game each in 2009-10 and 2010-11 was missing from the newer table and was filled from the older one. There was no 2004-05 season (lockout). Games before 2005 can end in ties, which count as half a win for both teams in the ratings; an overtime loss earns a standings point only from 1999-2000. A shootout win counts as a one-goal win, which is how the official score is recorded. The 2020 playoff bubble is treated as neutral-site. Before 1927 the champion shown is the winner of the NHL playoff, which then played a western champion for the Stanley Cup (those games are not in the data).

Franchise lineage follows the official records: Quebec Bulldogs → Hamilton Tigers → New York / Brooklyn Americans (folded 1942); Toronto Arenas → St. Patricks → Maple Leafs; Detroit Cougars → Falcons → Red Wings; Pittsburgh Pirates → Philadelphia Quakers (folded 1931); Ottawa Senators (original) → St. Louis Eagles (folded 1935); Montréal Wanderers (folded 1918) and Maroons (1925-38); Oakland / California Golden Seals → Cleveland Barons (merged into Minnesota 1978); Minnesota North Stars → Dallas Stars; Atlanta Flames → Calgary; Kansas City Scouts → Colorado Rockies → New Jersey Devils; Hartford Whalers → Carolina; Quebec Nordiques → Colorado Avalanche; Winnipeg Jets (1979-96) → Phoenix → Arizona Coyotes → Utah Hockey Club → Utah Mammoth; Atlanta Thrashers → Winnipeg Jets (2011-12). The modern Ottawa Senators (1992) are a different franchise from the original ones. Vegas (2017-18) and Seattle (2021-22) enter as new teams. 1994-95, 2012-13 and 2020-21 were shortened by lockouts or the pandemic, and 2019-20 stopped in March before a 24-team bubble playoff.

Team badges are plain colour tabs with the code, not logos. Source data can contain rare typos in scores or dates.
