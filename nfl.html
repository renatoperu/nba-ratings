How the ratings work

Every distinct date is one Glicko-2 rating period, so each rating change maps to the games played that day. Regular season and playoff games all count (switch any of them off in Settings).

Between seasons the rating carries over and the rating deviation (RD) resets to a smaller starting value, since the team's history is informative. If a franchise disappeared, all continuing teams are moved by the same amount so the league average is back at 1500. A new franchise enters at the rating that keeps the league average at 1500 (Settings lets you choose between that literal rule and first re-centring the continuing teams so entrants come in at exactly 1500).

Margin of victory enters through the game score: the winner scores 1 / (1 + e^(−margin / 14)). Home field is worth a rating edge that is estimated each season from the previous two regular seasons only, so nothing looks ahead. Neutral-site games (the 2020 bubble) get no edge.

"Win %" in Games is the pre-game probability the home team wins, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability.

How it works

Each simulated season starts from the records so far and plays every standings game (regular season) still without a result. Win probabilities come from the current ratings plus the home edge, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability when margin scoring is on. With rating uncertainty on, each season first draws every team's true strength from its rating ± RD, so early in the year the spread of outcomes is wide and it tightens as results come in.

The schedule has 17 games per team; any games missing from it are filled in with random opponents until you add them. Teams are ranked in each conference by record. Ties for a playoff place use the head-to-head record, then the record against the division, then the record within the conference, then point differential (the other official steps, such as common games and strength of victory, are skipped). The field is the four division winners plus three wild cards per conference; the top seed has a bye, the best seed hosts every round, the top seed meets the lowest survivor after the Wild Card round, and the Super Bowl is at a neutral site. Odds appear in the Championship odds table.

Import schedule or results

Paste or upload a CSV with the columns Date, Visitor, Pts, Home, Pts (one game per line). Plain date,away,home[,awayPts,homePts[,type]] rows work too, and so does a Type or Notes column (Playoffs). Team names, nicknames and abbreviations are all understood.

Importing merges: new games are added and scores fill in results for games you already have. Re-import any time to load new results.

Automatic updates

A scheduled job refreshes the schedule and results several times a day and publishes them with this app; the app merges them every time you open it. Results you type yourself are never overwritten, and a result you clear is filled in again from the file.

Glicko-2 parameters

RD at season start is 150 by default (a season is only 17 games, so ratings stay uncertain; 120–200 fit about equally well, 50 clearly worse). New franchises start at 350. Margin scales of 8 to 18 points made almost no difference, so 14 stays.

Model quality

Pre-game predictions of the home team winning, evaluated over every game that feeds the ratings. Log loss of a coin flip is 0.693.

Data and assumptions

Games from 1966 to 1998 come from the FiveThirtyEight nfl-elo-game data (every NFL and AFL game); 1999 to 2026 come from the nflverse nfldata games table, which matches the first source except for a few neutral-site games with home and away swapped. Only regular season and playoff games count: preseason games and the Playoff Bowl are excluded. The Super Bowl and games played abroad are treated as neutral-site. Ties count as half a win. 1982 and 1987 were shortened by strikes, 2020 had COVID changes, and the regular season has had 14, 16 or 17 games (1966–77, 1978–2020, 2021 on); the playoffs have had 4, 8, 10, 12 and 14 teams, and the simulator uses today's 14.

From 1966 to 1969 the NFL and the AFL were separate leagues that met only in the Super Bowl, so ratings of the two groups are linked by a single game a year. Franchise lineage: St. Louis → Phoenix → Arizona Cardinals; Baltimore → Indianapolis Colts; Boston → New England Patriots; San Diego → Los Angeles Chargers; Los Angeles → St. Louis → Los Angeles Rams; Oakland → Los Angeles → Oakland → Las Vegas Raiders; Houston Oilers → Tennessee Oilers → Titans; Redskins → Football Team → Commanders. Expansion: Saints 1967, Bengals 1968, Seahawks and Buccaneers 1976, Panthers and Jaguars 1995, Ravens 1996 (a new franchise, not the old Browns), Texans 2002. Conferences and divisions shown are today's.

Team badges are plain colour tabs with the code, not logos. Source data can contain rare typos in scores or dates.
