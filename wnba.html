How the ratings work

Every distinct date is one Glicko-2 rating period, so each rating change maps to the games played that day. Regular season, Commissioner’s Cup, Cup final, playoff games all count (switch any of them off in Settings).

Between seasons the rating carries over and the rating deviation (RD) resets to a smaller starting value, since the team's history is informative. If a franchise disappeared, all continuing teams are moved by the same amount so the league average is back at 1500. A new franchise enters at the rating that keeps the league average at 1500 (Settings lets you choose between that literal rule and first re-centring the continuing teams so entrants come in at exactly 1500).

Margin of victory enters through the game score: the winner scores 1 / (1 + e^(−margin / 10)). Home court is worth a rating edge that is estimated each season from the previous two regular seasons only, so nothing looks ahead. Neutral-site games (the 2020 bubble) get no edge.

"Win %" in Games is the pre-game probability the home team wins, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability.

How it works

Each simulated season starts from the records so far and plays every standings game (regular season and Commissioner’s Cup) still without a result. Win probabilities come from the current ratings plus the home edge, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability when margin scoring is on. With rating uncertainty on, each season first draws every team's true strength from its rating ± RD, so early in the year the spread of outcomes is wide and it tightens as results come in.

Any games missing from the schedule are filled in with random opponents until you add them. Teams are ranked by wins with ties broken at random; the real WNBA tiebreakers are not modelled. Playoff games are not simulated: the top 8 teams qualify.

Import schedule or results

On Basketball-Reference's WNBA season schedule pages use Share & Export → Get table as CSV, then upload the file or paste the text. Plain date,away,home[,awayPts,homePts[,type]] rows work too, and so does a Type or Notes column (Cup, Playoffs). Team names, nicknames and abbreviations are all understood.

Importing merges: new games are added and scores fill in results for games you already have. Re-import any time to load new results.

Automatic updates

A scheduled job refreshes the schedule and results several times a day and publishes them with this app; the app merges them every time you open it. Results you type yourself are never overwritten, and a result you clear is filled in again from the file.

Glicko-2 parameters

RD at season start is 100 by default (a grid over all 73,000 games found 75–150 about equally good; the best log loss was at 100). New franchises start at 350.

Model quality

Pre-game predictions of the home team winning, evaluated over every game that feeds the ratings. Log loss of a coin flip is 0.693.

Data and assumptions

2002–2025 games and the 2026 season come from ESPN schedules (via the wehoop / sportsdataverse data repository). All-Star games are excluded. Seasons 1997–2001 are not in this source, so every team starts 2002 at 1500 and the first two seasons use default home-court and margin values.

Franchise lineage: Orlando Miracle → Connecticut Sun; Utah Starzz → San Antonio Silver Stars/Stars → Las Vegas Aces; Detroit Shock → Tulsa Shock → Dallas Wings. Defunct: Charlotte Sting, Cleveland Rockers, Houston Comets, Miami Sol, Portland Fire (2000–02), Sacramento Monarchs. The 2026 Portland Fire is a new franchise. The 2020 season is treated as neutral-site (Florida bubble).

Team badges are plain colour tabs with the code, not logos. Source data can contain rare typos in scores or dates.
