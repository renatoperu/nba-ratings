How the ratings work

Every distinct date is one Glicko-2 rating period, so each rating change maps to the games played that day. Regular season, NBA Cup, Cup final, play-in and playoff games all count (switch any of them off in Settings).

Between seasons the rating carries over and the rating deviation (RD) resets to a smaller starting value, since the team's history is informative. If a franchise disappeared, all continuing teams are moved by the same amount so the league average is back at 1500. A new franchise enters at the rating that keeps the league average at 1500 (Settings lets you choose between that literal rule and first re-centring the continuing teams so entrants come in at exactly 1500).

Margin of victory enters through the game score: the winner scores 1 / (1 + e^(−margin / 10)). Home court is worth a rating edge that is estimated each season from the previous two regular seasons only, so nothing looks ahead. Neutral-site games (the 2020 bubble) get no edge.

"Win %" in Games is the pre-game probability the home team wins, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability.

How it works

Each simulated season starts from the records so far and plays every standings game (regular season and NBA Cup) still without a result. Win probabilities come from the current ratings plus the home edge, converted from Glicko-2's expected score to a true win probability when margin scoring is on. With rating uncertainty on, each season first draws every team's true strength from its rating ± RD, so early in the year the spread of outcomes is wide and it tightens as results come in.

The schedule has 80 games per team; the rest (Cup knockout and flex games) are filled in with random opponents until you add them. Teams are ranked in each conference by wins. Ties use the official order: head-to-head, division winner, conference record, then point differential (the record-against-playoff-teams steps are skipped, a few multi-team rules are simplified, and simulated margins are generic). The play-in and playoffs are simulated in the Championship odds table.

Import schedule or results

On Basketball-Reference's NBA_2027_games pages use Share & Export → Get table as CSV, then upload the file or paste the text. Plain date,away,home[,awayPts,homePts[,type]] rows work too, and so does a Type or Notes column (Cup, Play-In, Playoffs). Team names, nicknames and abbreviations are all understood.

Importing merges: new games are added and scores fill in results for games you already have. Re-import any time to load new results.

Automatic updates

A scheduled job refreshes the schedule and results several times a day and publishes them with this app; the app merges them every time you open it. Results you type yourself are never overwritten, and a result you clear is filled in again from the file.

Glicko-2 parameters

RD at season start is 100 by default (a grid over all 73,000 games found 75–150 about equally good; the best log loss was at 100). New franchises start at 350.

Model quality

Pre-game predictions of the home team winning, evaluated over every game that feeds the ratings. Log loss of a coin flip is 0.693.

Data and assumptions

1946-47 to 2014-15 games come from FiveThirtyEight's nbaallelo dataset; 2015-16 onwards from ESPN schedules (via the hoopR / sportsdataverse data repository). All-Star games are excluded. The ABA is not included, so Denver, Indiana, San Antonio and the Nets enter in 1976-77 as new teams, and the four ABA-merger teams count as entering together.

Franchise lineage follows the official records (for example Syracuse Nationals → Philadelphia 76ers, Minneapolis Lakers → Los Angeles Lakers, Charlotte Hornets 1988-2002 stay with Charlotte, New Orleans' original Hornets are the Pelicans, Seattle SuperSonics → Oklahoma City Thunder). Defunct franchises: Anderson, Baltimore Bullets, Chicago Stags, Cleveland Rebels, Denver Nuggets (1949-50), Detroit Falcons, Indianapolis Jets, Indianapolis Olympians, Pittsburgh Ironmen, Providence Steamrollers, Sheboygan Red Skins, St. Louis Bombers, Toronto Huskies, Waterloo Hawks, Washington Capitols.

Team badges are plain colour tabs with the code, not logos. Source data can contain rare typos in scores or dates.
